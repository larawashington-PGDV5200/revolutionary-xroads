# R3: Native-authored perspectives on land cession, 1763–1840 (agent research, 2026-11-03)

This is a source list, not an analysis. It has 30 sources, 5 collections and a mapping of Gwynne's source types. Treaty IDs (`T1795` and so on) join to `research/treaties_R1.csv`.
**Voice** says how directly the Native author speaks:
- *written*: a Native author wrote it.
- *recorded*: a speech written down by a colonial or US secretary or interpreter.
- *mediated*: dictated to, or edited by, a non-Native writer.
- *oral history*: recorded in the 20th century.
- *Nation today*: published by a present-day Nation or its institution.

Follow the brief's rule: quote and cite these sources, and don't paraphrase them as a Nation's view.

## Caveats
- **Recorded speeches are not transcripts.** They come through interpreters and secretaries, and several exist in more than one version (Little Turtle has 4, per M2). Tecumseh 1810 survives only as Harrison's clerks' copy. The famous "sell the air" line comes from later sources. Logan's Lament was transmitted by John Gibson.
- **Unverified:**
  - The Harkins date (Dec 1831, Vicksburg *Advocate & Register*, vs. 25 Feb 1832).
  - Peter Harris's exact wording.
  - ASP page numbers other than IA 1:356–57 and 1:570–71.
  - The volume number of the Aupaumut 1827 printing.
  - Where the original Cherokee women's petitions are held.
- **Newspaper flags.** Only Logan 1775 has a confirmed printing in 1770–1810. The "cand." rows are leads to search for in the HF news corpus (for example, "Corn-planter", "Little Turtle", "Six Nations"), not established reprints.
- **Oral-history access.** OU paused online access to R3-23 and R3-24 to consult source communities. Don't scrape or quote them without checking OU's current terms.
- **Pre-1784 anchors** (R3-01, R3-02) have no Royce number. Plot them as points, as R1 does.
